Six products joined the directory, all suggested by the community, and a seventh suggestion turned out to be a listing that needed updating rather than a new entry.

Two are conventional cloud CMSs at opposite ends of the market. **SignageStack** sells one plan with everything in it at EUR 11 per screen. **GlücksCast**, from a Nuremberg IT shop, offers a self-hosted licence alongside its hosted plans.

Two are built for a single trade. **DopeCast** does cannabis dispensary menu boards, with direct POS integrations that pull prices and stock every few minutes, and **CedMenu** designs restaurant menu boards to order and ships them on Android player boxes.

The last two are priced unlike anything else in the directory. **Better Signage** charges for storage instead of screens, so every plan from the free 50 MB tier upwards carries unlimited Apple TVs. **RedSquare** charges nothing at all: venues install a free player, approve what plays, and set a price per 10-second slot, and the platform takes a cut of each booking instead of a subscription.

**Signital** was already listed, but the suggestion that arrived with it carried enough detail to fill the entry out: a founding year, the Raspberry Pi, BrightSign and browser players that were missing from the platform list, the Enterprise tier, the volume pricing that starts at five screens, and a published screen count from its Samkaup and Olís deployments.

Two updates came in as pull requests from the people who build the products. **Fusion Signage** migrated its website, so its documentation link and news feed had both gone stale; the same change brings its pricing up to the current AUD plans and records its ISO 27001 certification. **GarlicSignage** was filed as cloud software, which undersold it: it is a self-hosted AGPL suite, a SMIL player plus the garlic-hub CMS, either of which can be deployed on its own.
