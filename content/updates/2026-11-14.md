## Compliance, with sources

The compliance section of a listing used to be five yes/no flags, and almost every listing had all five set to no. That said less about the vendors than about how hard the information is to find: certifications sit in website footers, trust centres, security pages and HIPAA pages that the homepage never links to.

Every active vendor website was checked for security certifications and privacy claims, 597 of 625 in all. 171 listings now carry at least one claim, drawn from 27 standards including SOC 1, 2 and 3, the ISO 27000 family, ISO 9001 and 14001, HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA, PCI DSS, Cyber Essentials, TISAX, CSA STAR, ENS, CRA, VPAT and WCAG.

Each claim records what kind of claim it is, because the standards work differently:

- Certified: an audited certificate or report is held, such as a SOC 2 report, an ISO certificate or Cyber Essentials.
- Compliant: the vendor declares compliance with a law or framework that has no certificate of its own. GDPR and HIPAA work this way. There is no HIPAA certificate, so a vendor offering a Business Associate Agreement is the strongest signal available.
- In progress: the vendor says an audit is under way.

Every badge on a product page now links to the vendor page that states the claim. Hovering it shows the detail (the SOC 2 report type, a BAA on offer, a certificate held by a parent company) and the month it was checked.

## What was left out

Only claims a vendor makes about itself count. Certificates held by a hosting provider ("hosted on AWS, which is ISO 27001 certified") belong to the host and are not listed. Neither are passing mentions: a privacy policy that lists GDPR rights without claiming compliance, or marketing copy calling a product "HIPAA-ready". Where a certificate belongs to a parent company, the listing says so.

Claims change over time, so treat each badge as a dated pointer to the vendor's own page.
