Seven products joined the directory, all suggested by the community:

- **Tilecast**, an open-source platform you host yourself with Docker Compose. Screens are managed from a browser app, and displays run a native player for Android TV devices or x86_64 Linux that keeps playing when the network drops.
- **Matrixign**, a Colombian CMS for operators who run screens in venues they don't own. Each client brand gets its own portal with content approval and proof-of-play reports, and players cover Android TV boxes, Windows kiosks, and holographic LED fans.
- **SCRIX**, a French tool for computer and phone shops. It replaces paper price cards with a label on each demo laptop, phone, or tablet, including French regulatory data such as the repairability index.
- **Lunixo**, an Austrian CMS built for advertising networks, with share of voice, playout caps, weather-triggered campaigns, and connections to programmatic buyers. It runs about 5,000 active licenses and ships its own Linux signage OS.
- **SignageWall**, a cloud CMS aimed at cafes, shops, offices, and schools, with 43 built-in content apps and native players that play from local storage when offline.
- **Vuubl**, a Texas CMS for small businesses, with a single all-features plan priced per screen and a browser or Android TV player.
- **Smart-Signage**, a French platform hosted on OVHcloud in France, with an AI-assisted content studio, meeting room booking, and e-paper shelf labels alongside regular screens.

Vega OS is now a platform in the directory. It's the operating system on newer Amazon Fire TV Sticks, which can't install Android apps, so it's listed separately from Fire OS. ScreenTinker is the first product with a Vega OS player.

Two listings were corrected after reports from the community. ScreenTinker now shows its REST API, single sign-on, and four more player platforms, and Q-Play now shows single sign-on.
